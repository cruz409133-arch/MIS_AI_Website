Correct AI Usage

AI has become increasingly popular in that past few years, and with it came various uses. Some of the correct uses of AI include helping with simple tasks like scheduling and reminders. Other uses that are also ethical are the usage of AI for cooking, shopping, or clarification. Through these uses of AI people can save time and make their lives easier without affecting others negatively.

AI's Critique and Perspective:

Your reasoning is a good starting point: you argue that AI can be used ethically when it helps people complete everyday tasks, saves time, and does not negatively affect others. The main weakness is that you treat certain categories of use as ethical without explaining the conditions that make them so.

What works well:

  • You give concrete examples. Scheduling, reminders, cooking, shopping, and clarification make your argument easy to understand.
  • You connect usefulness to a moral consideration. Your argument is not simply that AI is convenient; you also consider whether its use harms other people.
  • You distinguish between different uses of AI. This is more defensible than assuming that every use is either acceptable or unacceptable.

Where your reasoning needs strengthening

Convenience does not establish ethical acceptability

Saving time and making life easier explain why someone might want to use AI, but they do not, by themselves, establish that the use is ethical. Your argument needs a clearer bridge between “this is helpful” and “this is morally acceptable.”

For example, suppose an AI scheduling tool saves someone time but uses another person's private information without permission. The convenience would not resolve the concern about consent.

The category of task is not enough

Your paragraph implies that cooking or shopping is an ethical use simply because of the activity involved. A stronger approach would evaluate how the tool is used.

Consider these hypothetical cases:

  • Scheduling: Does the user have permission to share everyone's calendar information?
  • Cooking: Would the user check a suggestion before serving it to someone with a serious food allergy?
  • Shopping: Is the AI helping someone follow their own priorities, or steering them toward purchases without making that influence clear?
  • Clarification: Does the user treat the explanation as something to evaluate, or accept it without question?
These examples do not show that those uses are inherently wrong. They show that the same kind of task can raise different ethical questions depending on the circumstances.

“Without affecting others negatively” needs support

This is the most important assumption in your argument, but you state it as an established outcome. You have not yet shown that the examples avoid negative effects. It may also be too demanding to require no negative effects whatsoever. An alternative standard would be to ask whether the benefits justify the foreseeable risks, whether those risks are minimized, and whether the people affected are treated fairly.

An alternative perspective

Instead of dividing AI uses into “correct” and “incorrect” categories, judge each use by its purpose, methods, and consequences. Under this approach, an everyday use is not automatically ethical, and a complex use is not automatically unethical. The key questions are whether it respects privacy and consent, involves appropriate checking, and leaves someone accountable for the result.

A stronger version of your paragraph

AI has become increasingly popular in the past few years, with applications ranging from scheduling and reminders to cooking, shopping, and clarification. These uses can be ethically acceptable when they help people accomplish legitimate goals while respecting privacy, consent, and the interests of others. However, convenience alone does not make a use ethical. Users should consider the accuracy of AI-generated information, the information they share, and the possible consequences of acting on its suggestions. Used responsibly, AI can save time and make everyday life easier while limiting avoidable harm.

This preserves your central argument while making it conditional rather than assuming that ordinary tasks are necessarily harmless.

Incorrect AI Usage

Cheating
Students may use AI to answer exam questions when prohibited by the school honor code.
Plagiarism
Students may ask AI to create a piece of work and turn it in without citing that AI was used.
Falsification
Students may ask AI to generate false data and use it in their work.

With the rapid rise of AI, I believe it is important that the new generation understands the incorrect usage of AI. Which is why it is best to embrace this new change that is occuring and start teaching about AI and its uses early on, more specifically its incorrect usage. Most students are more likely to endulge in the incorrect usage of AI if they aren't familiar so stopping these habits at an early age will be more beneficial to them. AI will continue to be a part of our lives and it is in our best interest to integrate classes that teach the youth about it.
